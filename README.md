# Mathematics Roadmap
## From Basics to Mastery (Arithmetic to Graduate-Level Mathematics)

This roadmap is designed for someone who wants to learn mathematics properly, build deep understanding, and grow from absolute beginner to advanced level with confidence.

The goal is not just to memorize formulas. The goal is to learn how to think logically, solve problems, write proofs, build intuition, connect ideas across topics, and apply mathematics to real problems.

Mathematics is a ladder. Every rung supports the next one. If you climb carefully, nothing is out of reach.

## 1. How to Use This Roadmap

- Start at the top and move step by step.
- Do not skip fundamentals, even if they feel easy. Fast review is fine. Skipping is not.
- Do the problems yourself. Reading solutions is not the same as solving.
- After every phase, take a self-test and build a small project.
- Review old topics often. Mathematics is cumulative.
- If something feels difficult, slow down instead of rushing.
- If you are already advanced in some areas, use the exit criteria to test yourself and skip ahead.
- Choose one specialization later and go deeper there.

### A good rule
- Learn it
- Work examples
- Solve problems
- Explain it in your own words
- Review it later
- Use it in a project or a harder topic

## 2. Learning Principles

### What actually works
- Consistency matters more than motivation.
- Do math every day, even if only for 30 minutes.
- Problem solving is the main skill. Reading is only support.
- Struggle before you look at hints. The struggle is where learning happens.
- Write solutions in full sentences and clear steps.
- Check every answer. Plug it back in or test it with a special case.
- Learn the "why" behind every formula.
- Draw pictures and graphs whenever possible.
- Teach what you learn, even to an empty room.
- Keep an error log and review it weekly.
- Spaced repetition works. Revisit topics after 1 day, 1 week, 1 month.
- Mixed practice works better than blocked practice.

### What to avoid
- Memorizing formulas without understanding them
- Watching videos passively without solving problems
- Jumping to advanced topics with weak foundations
- Looking at the solution too quickly
- Skipping the proofs and derivations
- Avoiding hard problems
- Comparing your speed to others
- Studying only when you feel motivated
- Trusting a calculator more than your reasoning

### The four levels of understanding
1. **Recall**: you can state the definition or formula.
2. **Procedure**: you can carry out the method on a standard problem.
3. **Understanding**: you can explain why it works and when it fails.
4. **Creation**: you can adapt it to a new problem or prove it yourself.

Aim for level 3 on everything and level 4 on the core topics.

### The Feynman check
- Pick a topic.
- Explain it simply on paper as if teaching a beginner.
- Mark where your explanation gets vague.
- Go back and fix those gaps.

### How to read a math textbook
- Read with a pencil and paper.
- Before reading a theorem's proof, try to prove it yourself.
- After each definition, write your own example and a non-example.
- After each theorem, ask what happens if one hypothesis is removed.
- Do at least half of the exercises. Do all the hard ones you can.

### How to handle being stuck
- Re-read the problem. Restate it in your own words.
- Try a smaller or simpler case.
- Draw a diagram.
- Work backward from the goal.
- List what you know and what you need.
- Look for a similar problem you have solved.
- Take a break. Your brain keeps working in the background.
- Only then, look at a hint, not the full solution.

```python
# SymPy: symbolic derivative and integral
import sympy as sp
x = sp.symbols("x")
f = x**2 * sp.sin(x)
print(sp.diff(f, x))
print(sp.integrate(f, x))

# NumPy: solve a linear system and find eigenvalues
import numpy as np
A = np.array([[2.0, 1.0], [1.0, 3.0]])
b = np.array([1.0, 2.0])
print(np.linalg.solve(A, b))
print(np.linalg.eig(A))

# Monte Carlo estimate of pi
import random
n = 1_000_000
inside = sum(1 for _ in range(n) if random.random()**2 + random.random()**2 <= 1)
print(4 * inside / n)

# Newton's method for sqrt(2)
def newton(f, df, x0, steps=10):
    x = x0
    for _ in range(steps):
        x = x - f(x) / df(x)
    return x
print(newton(lambda x: x**2 - 2, lambda x: 2*x, 1.0))
```

### Rules for using code
- Solve by hand first, verify with code.
- Test code with cases where you know the answer.
- Watch for floating-point error.
- Use code to explore patterns, then prove them.
- Never accept a numerical result as a proof.

## 35. Key Formulas Reference

A quick reference. Understand where each formula comes from before you memorize it.

### Algebra
- Quadratic formula: `x = (-b ± sqrt(b^2 - 4ac)) / (2a)`
- Discriminant: `D = b^2 - 4ac` (positive: two real roots, zero: one root, negative: complex roots)
- Difference of squares: `a^2 - b^2 = (a - b)(a + b)`
- Perfect square: `(a ± b)^2 = a^2 ± 2ab + b^2`
- Sum and difference of cubes: `a^3 ± b^3 = (a ± b)(a^2 ∓ ab + b^2)`
- Binomial theorem: `(a + b)^n = sum of C(n, k) a^(n-k) b^k`
- Exponent rules: `a^m a^n = a^(m+n)`, `(a^m)^n = a^(mn)`, `a^(-n) = 1/a^n`
- Log rules: `log(ab) = log a + log b`, `log(a^n) = n log a`, `log_b(a) = ln a / ln b`
- Arithmetic series: `S = n(a1 + an)/2`
- Geometric series: `S = a(1 - r^n)/(1 - r)`, and `a/(1 - r)` if `|r| < 1`
- Compound interest: `A = P(1 + r/n)^(nt)`, continuous: `A = P e^(rt)`

### Geometry
- Triangle area: `(1/2) b h`, Heron: `sqrt(s(s-a)(s-b)(s-c))`
- Circle: circumference `2 pi r`, area `pi r^2`
- Pythagorean theorem: `a^2 + b^2 = c^2`
- Distance: `sqrt((x2 - x1)^2 + (y2 - y1)^2)`
- Midpoint: `((x1 + x2)/2, (y1 + y2)/2)`
- Sphere: volume `(4/3) pi r^3`, surface area `4 pi r^2`
- Cone: volume `(1/3) pi r^2 h`
- Cylinder: volume `pi r^2 h`
- Polygon interior angle sum: `(n - 2) * 180` degrees
- Euler's polyhedron formula: `V - E + F = 2`

### Trigonometry
- `sin^2 x + cos^2 x = 1`
- `1 + tan^2 x = sec^2 x`
- `sin(a ± b) = sin a cos b ± cos a sin b`
- `cos(a ± b) = cos a cos b ∓ sin a sin b`
- `sin 2x = 2 sin x cos x`
- `cos 2x = cos^2 x - sin^2 x = 1 - 2 sin^2 x = 2 cos^2 x - 1`
- Law of sines: `a / sin A = b / sin B = c / sin C`
- Law of cosines: `c^2 = a^2 + b^2 - 2ab cos C`
- Euler's formula: `e^(i x) = cos x + i sin x`
- De Moivre: `(cos x + i sin x)^n = cos(nx) + i sin(nx)`
- Radians: `180 degrees = pi radians`

### Calculus
- Definition of derivative: `f'(x) = lim (h -> 0) [f(x + h) - f(x)] / h`
- Power rule: `d/dx x^n = n x^(n-1)`
- Product rule: `(fg)' = f'g + fg'`
- Quotient rule: `(f/g)' = (f'g - fg')/g^2`
- Chain rule: `(f(g(x)))' = f'(g(x)) g'(x)`
- `d/dx sin x = cos x`, `d/dx cos x = -sin x`, `d/dx tan x = sec^2 x`
- `d/dx e^x = e^x`, `d/dx ln x = 1/x`, `d/dx a^x = a^x ln a`
- `d/dx arcsin x = 1/sqrt(1 - x^2)`, `d/dx arctan x = 1/(1 + x^2)`
- Fundamental theorem: `integral from a to b of f'(x) dx = f(b) - f(a)`
- Integration by parts: `integral u dv = uv - integral v du`
- Power integral: `integral x^n dx = x^(n+1)/(n+1) + C` for `n != -1`
- L'Hopital: if `lim f/g` is `0/0` or `inf/inf`, then `lim f/g = lim f'/g'`
- Taylor series: `f(x) = sum of f^(n)(a)/n! (x - a)^n`
- Maclaurin: `e^x = sum x^n/n!`, `sin x = sum (-1)^n x^(2n+1)/(2n+1)!`, `1/(1 - x) = sum x^n`
- Arc length: `integral sqrt(1 + (f'(x))^2) dx`
- Volume by disks: `pi integral (f(x))^2 dx`

### Multivariable and vector calculus
- Gradient: `grad f = (f_x, f_y, f_z)`
- Directional derivative: `D_u f = grad f . u`
- Lagrange condition: `grad f = lambda grad g`
- Change of variables: `dA = |J| du dv`
- Polar: `dA = r dr d theta`; spherical: `dV = rho^2 sin(phi) d rho d phi d theta`
- Green's theorem: `closed integral P dx + Q dy = double integral (Q_x - P_y) dA`
- Stokes' theorem: `closed integral F . dr = surface integral (curl F) . dS`
- Divergence theorem: `closed surface integral F . dS = triple integral (div F) dV`

### Linear algebra
- Matrix product: `(AB)_ij = sum over k of A_ik B_kj`
- `det(AB) = det(A) det(B)`, `(AB)^(-1) = B^(-1) A^(-1)`, `(AB)^T = B^T A^T`
- Eigenvalue equation: `A v = lambda v`, characteristic polynomial `det(A - lambda I) = 0`
- Trace is the sum of eigenvalues; determinant is the product of eigenvalues
- Rank-nullity: `rank(A) + nullity(A) = n`
- Projection onto `u`: `(v . u / u . u) u`
- Normal equations: `A^T A x = A^T b`
- SVD: `A = U Sigma V^T`
- Cauchy-Schwarz: `|u . v| <= ||u|| ||v||`
- Spectral theorem: a real symmetric matrix is orthogonally diagonalizable

### Probability and statistics
- Conditional probability: `P(A|B) = P(A and B)/P(B)`
- Bayes: `P(A|B) = P(B|A) P(A) / P(B)`
- Expectation: `E[X] = sum x p(x)` or `integral x f(x) dx`
- Variance: `Var(X) = E[X^2] - (E[X])^2`
- Linearity: `E[aX + bY] = a E[X] + b E[Y]`
- Binomial: `P(X = k) = C(n, k) p^k (1 - p)^(n-k)`, mean `np`, variance `np(1 - p)`
- Poisson: `P(X = k) = e^(-lambda) lambda^k / k!`, mean and variance `lambda`
- Normal density: `(1/(sigma sqrt(2 pi))) e^(-(x - mu)^2/(2 sigma^2))`
- Exponential: `f(x) = lambda e^(-lambda x)`, mean `1/lambda`
- Chebyshev: `P(|X - mu| >= k sigma) <= 1/k^2`
- Central limit theorem: sample mean is approximately `N(mu, sigma^2/n)`
- Z-score: `z = (x - mu)/sigma`
- Confidence interval for a mean: `x_bar ± t* s/sqrt(n)`
- Least squares slope: `b = sum (x - x_bar)(y - y_bar) / sum (x - x_bar)^2`
- Entropy: `H = -sum p log p`

### Discrete math and number theory
- Permutations: `P(n, k) = n!/(n - k)!`
- Combinations: `C(n, k) = n!/(k!(n - k)!)`
- Stars and bars: `C(n + k - 1, k - 1)`
- Inclusion-exclusion for two sets: `|A ∪ B| = |A| + |B| - |A ∩ B|`
- Handshake lemma: sum of degrees `= 2|E|`
- Euler's formula (planar graphs): `V - E + F = 2`
- Fibonacci closed form: `F_n = (phi^n - psi^n)/sqrt(5)`
- Fermat's little theorem: `a^(p-1) ≡ 1 (mod p)` for prime `p` and `p` not dividing `a`
- Euler's theorem: `a^phi(n) ≡ 1 (mod n)` when `gcd(a, n) = 1`
- Euler's totient: `phi(n) = n * product of (1 - 1/p)` over primes `p | n`
- Bezout: `gcd(a, b) = ax + by` for some integers `x, y`
- Master theorem (divide and conquer): `T(n) = a T(n/b) + f(n)`

### Differential equations
- Separable: `dy/dx = g(x) h(y)` gives `integral dy/h(y) = integral g(x) dx`
- Linear first order: `y' + P y = Q`, integrating factor `e^(integral P dx)`
- Constant coefficients: `a y'' + b y' + c y = 0`, characteristic equation `a r^2 + b r + c = 0`
- Laplace transform: `L{f} = integral from 0 to infinity of e^(-st) f(t) dt`
- Heat equation: `u_t = k u_xx`; wave equation: `u_tt = c^2 u_xx`; Laplace: `u_xx + u_yy = 0`

### Analysis and complex analysis
- Epsilon-delta limit: for every `epsilon > 0` there is `delta > 0` such that `0 < |x - a| < delta` implies `|f(x) - L| < epsilon`
- Cauchy-Riemann: `u_x = v_y` and `u_y = -v_x`
- Cauchy integral formula: `f(a) = (1/(2 pi i)) closed integral f(z)/(z - a) dz`
- Residue theorem: `closed integral f dz = 2 pi i * sum of residues`
- Euler identity: `e^(i pi) + 1 = 0`
- Gamma function: `Gamma(n) = (n - 1)!`
- Basel problem: `sum 1/n^2 = pi^2/6`
- Stirling: `n! ≈ sqrt(2 pi n) (n/e)^n`

### Algebra (abstract)
- Lagrange's theorem: order of a subgroup divides the order of the group
- Orbit-stabilizer: `|G| = |orbit| * |stabilizer|`
- First isomorphism theorem: `G/ker(phi) ≅ im(phi)`
- Class equation: `|G| = |Z(G)| + sum of [G : C(x)]`
- Degree tower law: `[L : K] = [L : M][M : K]`

### Important constants
- `pi ≈ 3.14159265`
- `e ≈ 2.71828183`
- `phi (golden ratio) ≈ 1.61803399`
- `sqrt(2) ≈ 1.41421356`
- `sqrt(3) ≈ 1.73205081`
- `ln 2 ≈ 0.69314718`
- `gamma (Euler-Mascheroni) ≈ 0.57721566`

## 37. Final Notes

### Remember
- Mathematics rewards patience far more than speed.
- Confusion is a normal part of learning, not a sign of failure.
- Everyone who is good at mathematics has struggled, often for a long time.
- Understanding comes in waves. A topic that makes no sense today may click next month.
- You do not need to be a genius. You need curiosity, consistency, and honest practice.
- Mistakes you find yourself teach more than any lecture.
- Beautiful ideas are waiting at every level. Enjoy them.

### A simple way to measure progress
Every month, ask yourself:
- What can I do now that I could not do a month ago?
- Which problems that stumped me before are now easy?
- Where am I still weak, and what is my plan to fix it?
- What did I build, prove, or explain this month?

### If you feel stuck or discouraged
- Return to an easier topic and rebuild confidence.
- Try a different explanation, book, or video.
- Study with a friend or join a community.
- Take a short break, then return.
- Solve a few problems you know you can do, then try one harder one.
- Remember why you started.
